# 0289. Sticky clients, by what the other APs hear

- Status: Proposed
- Date: 2026-11-02
- Proposed by: Griff: "our APs should compare notes about the rssi of the clients under them. and then if a client is over say 4 or 5 db threshold it should force disco that sticky client and put on a hold down of 10 seconds or something"; then, of the client's own reports (802.11k), "that only works if they support 802.11k, I think one way AP to client rssi is good enough", and "k as an enhancement". Written up by Claude
- Refines: 0138, 0207
- Refined by: 0292, which moves the weak signal unset from -75 dBm to -80

## Context

- **Sticky (0138) moves a client by how its own AP hears it alone.** A client under `sticky_signal` (-80 dBm unless set, 0292; -75 before it) for three looks, where the network has a better place, is told to go, and put off for two and a half seconds if it stays. Whether the place it is told of is better is not known: it is any of the network's places hostapd lists.
- **The Living Room TV, 2026-11-02.** It sat on the Office AP's 5 GHz for eight hours at -96 to -97 dBm, 32% of what was sent to it sent again, with the Living Room AP in the same room. Steering was off on every network. Aeolus's disconnect (0107) put it off; it was back on the same BSS within a second, without a scan. Put off again with a ban of sixty seconds on both of the Office AP's BSSes of its network, it was on the Living Room AP seven seconds later, at -68 dBm and VHT-MCS 8.
- **No other AP had any reading of it.** A client that holds on sends no probes, and usteer's view of it, on the Living Room AP, was the Office AP's alone. A client that does scan sends probes on each channel it visits, and each AP on such a channel hears them, with their signal: hostapd says each probe and authentication to its listeners, aeolus-steer among them on a steered network.
- **usteer already shares such signals between APs** (`get_clients`), but says nothing of how old each is, and 0138 has usteer steer nothing. RRM's hellos (0073) already go between Aeolus APs over the wire, signed, and carry what the steering daemon reads of them (0207): their radios' loads.
- **A client's own report of what it hears (802.11k beacon reports, 0208) needs a client that gives them.** Griff: the APs' own readings are enough, and the client's report is to come as an enhancement.
- **A C-360's radios share the middle of their BSSIDs:** its 5 GHz network is 36:86:2d:ef:2f:c0, its 2.4 GHz 36:86:2d:ef:2f:d0. The middle alone names the AP, not the radio.

## Decision

- **Each AP keeps how strongly it hears the clients it does not serve, by their probes and authentications,** on each steered network whose own neighbour report it knows: per client, per band, the latest signal, its time, and the middle of the radio's BSSIDs. A reading holds for thirty seconds. A client that joins the AP is no longer among them. aeolus-steer writes them each look, to `/var/run/aeolus/steer.probed.json`: the latest first, at most 64.
- **The APs tell one another, a message with each hello.** aeolus-rrm reads that file (not older than fifteen seconds), ages each reading by the file's age, and sends `{t: 'probed', clients: [[mac, band, middle, signal, age]]}`, signed with RRM's key, to every Aeolus AP it knows, by hellos or in the air, not to its neighbours alone: a client's best AP may be one this AP is not among the nearest of (the Living Room AP and the C260 exchange no hellos, each the other's fourth). The message is fitted to one frame, the oldest readings left out: 22 readings fit in 1,357 bytes. A hello is unchanged.
- **What the others say is kept for aeolus-steer:** each message once, by RRM's freshness rule; each reading by when it was heard, by this AP's clock; written to `/var/run/aeolus/steer.probed.near.json` whenever a message comes. Readings older than thirty seconds are let go; a file not written for a minute says nothing.
- **Sticky moves a client another AP hears better** (`why: better`): one heard here under the network's floor (`steering.sticky_floor`, -65 dBm unless set), that another AP of the network hears on the same band by the margin or more (`steering.sticky_margin`, 5 dB unless set), by a reading not older than thirty seconds, at two looks running, where hostapd lists that AP's network on that band. The place is matched by the middle of its BSSID and the band of its operating class together.
  - One that takes 802.11v is told to go, with those places named, the most strongly heard first, disassociation imminent; and put off five seconds later if it is still there and not heard above the floor by 3 dB.
  - One that takes no request is put off at once.
- **A client may say it, as an enhancement (802.11k).** A client weak here, under sticky's weak signal (`sticky_signal`, -80 dBm unless set, 0292; -75 before it), that no other AP has heard on its band lately, and that gives beacon reports (its RM Enabled Capabilities, as 0208 reads them), is asked what it hears, once it has been on five seconds; then five minutes later, and twice as long after each asking that did not move it (it said it hears nowhere better, or said nothing), up to two hours; every five minutes again once it is asked to go, or is on another of the network's bands here. Those are the clients the weak rule would put off with no place known to be better; one heard under the floor and no weaker is good where it is (-70 is good), and was asked for nothing: on the Living Room AP on 2026-11-03 the TV at -68 and another client were asked every five minutes, four channels each. Asked: a beacon request a channel, its own channel first and then those of the network's places on its band elsewhere, at most four, each with its 20 MHz operating class, of the network's SSID, passive where it can (0208's mode).
  - **What it says comes as hostapd's `beacon-report` notification** to aeolus-steer, which listens to the steered network already; hostapd does not wait for it, and nothing waits for the report. Whoever asked, aeolus-live's own askings (0208) among them, what a client on the network says is taken: of this AP by its own BSSID, and of the other BSSes it measured, each for thirty seconds. A report the client would not or could not make, or whose RCPI says no signal, is none; RCPI is read as 0208 reads it, the signal in dBm as a signed byte where a client writes it so.
  - **A place it hears by the margin more strongly than it says it hears this AP is a reading for the same rule** (`why: better`): set only against what it said of this AP, measured the same way, its own channel being asked first. Where it said nothing of this AP lately, what it says of the others makes no place, and the weak rule covers a client that is truly weak. What a client hears of an AP is of the AP's sending, which is louder than the client's own by 5 to 10 dB: set against how this AP hears the client, it would read high, and move clients near the floor that are better where they are. A place is matched by its whole BSSID, which a beacon report names, and must be on the client's band. The AP named in the record is the one RRM knows by that radio, by its middle and band; a place of an AP RRM does not know is a place all the same, and the record names no AP.
  - **It fails open:** a client that does not answer, or a hostapd that does not take the request, leaves the client as it was, to the weak rule.
- **The weak rule stays** (`why: weak`), for a client no other AP hears, as a client that holds on: under `sticky_signal` for three looks, where the network has a better place.
- **Put off, a client is kept off for the hold down** (`steering.sticky_hold_down`, ten seconds unless set), by hostapd's own ban, on the network's BSS it was on; and on every other band of the network on this AP, where its place is on another AP: always for `better`, and for `weak` where no lower band of the network on this AP is a place. Put off one BSS alone, the TV was back on it within a second.
- **0138's guards hold for both:** once in five minutes a client at most; one that comes straight back is left alone for half an hour, and twice as long each time, up to a day; an AP's own test client is never steered; hostapd never waits for the daemon; nothing is acted on that is not known to be so now.
- **A client put off that comes back within ten minutes came back,** on the band it was put off, or on any of the network's bands here where it was kept off all of them: counted as `back`, and left alone as 0138 has it (half an hour, twice as long each time, up to a day), though its asking already ended as `left` a minute on. That is said in a record of its own, asking nothing (`asked: none`), which the manager does not count as a second asking. One that went where it was sent, a lower band here, did not come back.
- **The weak rule puts a client off only where there is a sign it has somewhere better:** a lower band of the network here; or another AP of the network heard it, by its probes on any band, within the last day. A client no other AP hears is tried once: the weak rule's putting it off is kept as its try (`tried`), and it is not put off by the weak rule again for a week (BACKS_FOR), however long it takes to come back, nor where it has come back within that week, until another AP hears it. The heard-better rule and what a client says (802.11k) are as before.
- **Asked what it hears, a client is given ten seconds to say it** before the weak rule puts it off: asked at its second look, it would otherwise be put off at its third, before what it said had had its two looks. A client that says nothing is put off a look later than it would have been.
- **Each asking says why,** in its record: `why` (`weak` or `better`), and for `better`, `elsewhere`, the AP that heard it more strongly and how strongly. The AP's report says each sticky network's `margin`, `floor` and `hold_down` beside `weak`. The manager keeps `why` and the other AP with each asking (migration 36), and `GET /v1/steering` lists them, the other AP by name where the caller may view it.
- **The settings are three fields of the network,** beside `sticky` and `sticky_signal`, each with its bounds: margin 1 to 20 dB, floor -90 to -40 dBm, hold down 1 to 60 seconds. The renderer writes them to the daemon's section as one entry a sticky network, `list sticky_with '<network> margin=5 floor=-65 hold_down=10'`, every one of them, so that the check (0039) holds the entry to the intent exactly; `sticky` keeps its own entry as before. A setting the daemon does not know, or out of its bounds, is as unset.
- **In the UI,** the network's "Roaming and steering" fields gain the three numbers, and sticky's switch is "Move sticky clients"; the latest askings say why, and the AP that heard the client better, with its signal.

## Consequences

- **Fixed in v0.234.0: the file names.** v0.232.0 and v0.233.0 wrote each AP's readings to `/var/run/aeolus/probes.json`, the prober's own file (0059), every five seconds. The agent then sometimes read them as the prober's clients, and the manager refused that state report (`cannot unmarshal array into stateReport.clients.0`), on every AP from 22:25 CST 2026-11-02; the prober's DHCP watch was missing from those reports too. The readings are now `steer.probed.json` and `steer.probed.near.json`, and the agent leaves out of a report any client that is not one.

- **A client is weighed by how each AP hears it, and needs nothing of 802.11k.** The readings are the APs' of the client, uplink, as Griff asked: they differ by each AP's receiver and antennas, which the margin is to cover. The C-260's ath10k radio has read clients low in the lab, unexplained; its reading of a client is then less often the better one.
- **A client that sends no probes is weighed by what it says it hears, where it gives beacon reports and says it of this AP too, and else by its own AP alone,** by the weak rule, as before. That is the TV of the context, at -97: with sticky on, it would have been put off, and kept off both of the Office AP's bands for ten seconds.
- **Readings come only from steered networks:** an AP whose network is not steered hears no probes for it, and says none.
- **Without RRM there are no readings to share,** and sticky is as before.
- **A ban longer than 2.5 seconds is felt by a client with nowhere better to go:** wpa_supplicant, refused three times running, gave the network up for ten seconds (0138). The weak rule's client has a place listed, not one known to be better; one that comes back is left alone for half an hour, and longer each time, so a client at the far end of a house is put off a few times in all.
- **well_env_switch, 2026-11-03.** With sticky turned on for Home_Wi-Fi_IoT at 00:25 CST, the weak rule put off well_env_switch (84:0d:8e:73:9a:f7), an ESP32 the Shed AP heard at -93 and -94 and no other AP had ever heard, at 00:26:42, 00:32:23 and 00:37:51. It takes no 802.11v request; put off, it rejoined about five and a half minutes later (00:32:11, 00:37:51) whatever the ban's length, a ban of one second (change 619) as well as ten, and was weak again at once, put off twelve seconds after it was let on. Each asking ended as `left` at sixty seconds, never `back`, so it never had its rest, and was put off every five or six minutes. Sticky was paused there (change 620) until this was fixed: its coming back within ten minutes is counted as back, and, being heard by no other AP, it is put off once and then left alone.
- **Counted by its coming back, it was tried twice all the same.** With v0.236.0, and sticky on again there (change 621, 01:35 CST), the weak rule put it off at 01:36:38, as its one try. It rejoined at 01:47:06, ten and a half minutes on, past the ten that count as coming back, and was put off again at 01:47:18 as a first try; it rejoined at 01:52:46, five and a half minutes on, was counted back, and was left alone. Its delay before joining again runs from five and a half to ten and a half minutes. So it is the try that is counted for a lone client, not its coming back; the ten minutes stay for 0138's rest.
- **An AP with an older agent renders no `sticky_with`,** and the check refuses its config where a network has sticky on, until its agent is the manager's own, a minute later, as 0138 had it. An older agent's report and records say no `why`; the manager takes them as before.
- **Not done here:**
  - Turning sticky on for a network is Griff's: it turns on 802.11k and 802.11v, which restarts the network's Wi-Fi on each AP.
  - 0207's loads by BSSID middle named one radio of a C-360 for all three, so steering's order of places by load could take one band's load for another's: since fixed, by middle and band (0207).
- **Asked, a client measures off its channel** for 120 TU a channel, four at most, at most once in five minutes while it is weak here, and less often each time it stays: a few hundred milliseconds away. One in the middle of something that will not wait may take a report as a slight; that is what the five minutes are for.
- **What came back, what the weak rule tried, when another AP last heard each client, and each rest are kept from one run of the daemon to the next,** with the clients' bands, in its file on tmpfs (`steer.known.json`, by the calendar's clock): an update of the agent, which starts the daemon again, forgets none of it (four releases on the night of 2026-11-02 would each have cost the well switch another putting off and five and a half minutes off the air). A reboot clears the file; a client no other AP hears is then put off once more. Each is read back checked, and only while it holds: a coming back or a try within a week, a hearing within a day, a rest to come and no longer than a day. A file from before 0289, its clients' bands alone, is read as before.
  - **How often each came back and was sent down, and until when each is left down, are kept too** (2026-11-05). Without them, each start of the daemon counted from one again: a client that keeps coming back was left alone half an hour, not twice as long each time, as every change of config or update of the agent starts the daemon. An ESP on the Shed (44:1d:64:bf:d4:98), weak there, was put off 24 times in three days, half an hour or an hour apart, coming back each time. A count is read back from 1 to 64, within a week and not to come. Checked by agent/test/steer.uc: a client that keeps coming back, the daemon started again after each time, is put off 7 times in a day and a half, as without the restarts; it was 72.
- **Checked:** the steering daemon's judgement, by agent/test/steer.uc, played with the real ucode (25.12's, on talos): a client heard 8 dB better elsewhere is told to go at the second look and goes; none is asked when heard well here, for a reading 40 seconds old, on another band, short of the margin, or of a place hostapd does not list; one that takes no request is put off at once, kept off both bands for the network's hold down; one told to go that stays is put off five seconds on; one that comes straight back is left alone. The messages, by agent/test/probed.uc. The 802.11k enhancement, by agent/test/steer.uc too: a client at -78 no other AP hears, that gives beacon reports, is asked once it has been on five seconds, of its own channel and its place elsewhere, then 5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 minutes on and every two hours after, while one at -68 is never asked; asked to go and back later, it is asked five seconds on and five minutes after that; none heard well here, none that gives no reports, none another AP hears, none on a network with no place elsewhere on its band, and no test client, is asked; one that says it hears a place 9 dB better than it hears this AP is told to go there at the second look, that AP named; 3 dB better than it hears this AP, though this AP hears it 11 dB weaker, it is not; nor one that names a place 9 dB over how this AP hears it and says nothing of this AP, until it does; nor for a report 31 seconds old, a report it would not make, one of a BSS that is no place, of a client not on the network there; a place of an AP RRM does not know is a place, the record naming no AP. hostapd's notification as agent/test/measure.uc reads it. The weak rule's signs, by agent/test/steer.uc as well: a client weak at -93 that no other AP hears is put off once, comes back five and a half minutes later (its asking having ended as left), is counted back, and is not put off again twelve seconds on, five minutes on, or in four hours; one another AP heard two hours before is put off again after its rest; one whose place is a lower band here is not held back by having come back before; a client like the TV, weak, heard by no other AP and come back before, goes by what it says, the weak rule waiting for it, and one that says nothing is put off a look later. A client back ten and a half minutes after its try is not put off again, fifteen seconds, five minutes or an hour on, nor in the hour after a restart from what was kept, and is put off once another AP hears it. Kept across a restart: a lone client that came back before the daemon started again is not put off in the four hours after (without what was kept, it is within the minute); a file from before 0289 reads as before, and what is past its time or not well said is not taken. Both daemons compile with that ucode. The render cases, the check, the report, the store and the API, by their tests. Not yet on an AP: steering is on nowhere in the lab, and turning it on is Griff's call.
